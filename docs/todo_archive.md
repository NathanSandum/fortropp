## [x] 5. Add custom domain verification (TLS issuance split out to item 16)
`values/domain.rs` `Domain` type is just a validated string stored on `Website` — no DNS-ownership verification or TLS-certificate-issuance status/UI exists.

Host-header spoofing itself is a smaller risk than originally framed: in prod, Cloudflare routes by configured hostname, and a spoofed Host only gets the attacker another fortropp site's content under our address, not under the victim's domain. The real risk is unverified *claims* — squatting and dangling-DNS takeover.

**Design (settled 2026-10-02): subdomains always verified, apexes verified on conflict.**

"Apex" = hostname equal to its registrable domain (eTLD+1 via Public Suffix List, e.g. `psl` crate — so `foo.co.uk` is an apex). "Subdomain" = anything longer, **including `www`**.

- **Go-live gate (all claims)**: a claimed hostname only serves once DNS points at us and the TLS cert issues (Cloudflare for SaaS custom-hostname validation).
- **Gate model (settled 2026-10-03, supersedes the earlier CNAME-as-proof design)**: what fortropp owns is the list of hostnames and the rule for who passes — each domain is **gated** (awaiting proof) or **admitted** (registered with Cloudflare, has a cert, routed). The user's DNS records are theirs, not a product concept. Pointing and proving are separate jobs:
  - **Pointing (instruction only)**: every domain points at one shared edge name (e.g. `edge.<user-content-domain>`; apex via ALIAS/flattened CNAME). Same for every site — no per-site CNAME targets in the domain feature.
  - **Proof (the only rule)**: a gated domain passes by publishing its TXT token at `_fortropp-challenge.<full hostname>`, never at the parent domain; the TXT record must sit at that exact name (a CNAME at the challenge name doesn't count). Verifying one hostname proves nothing about its parent or siblings. A wildcard can't open anyone's gate, since it can't put a token at someone else's challenge name.
  - Cost: subdomains need two records (pointing + TXT) instead of one — Vercel-style. Gain: one kind of proof everywhere, no `www → @` special case, no `cname_points_to` in the backend.
- **Subdomains: gated** (must prove with TXT) unless they inherit from their root — see the umbrella rules below.
- **Apexes: admitted by default**, gated only on conflict.
- **Root umbrella (settled 2026-10-03)** — goal: `a.com` + `www.a.com` with zero proof. "Root" always means the registrable domain (PSL eTLD+1), never an intermediate name (`a.b.c.com`'s root is `c.com`). Holding `a.com` implicitly claims the whole domain:
  1. **Unproven umbrella**: an account holding `a.com` (admitted, unproven) gets its *own* subdomains admitted free, as long as no other account has ever held any hostname under `a.com` (checked against `hostname_claims`, matched by registrable domain).
  2. **Contest**: the first claim by another account anywhere under `a.com` (pending claims included) revokes the unproven umbrella — the holder's free subdomains drop back to awaiting proof. `a.com` itself keeps serving (its own exact-name admission is unaffected). Griefing cost accepted: a bogus claim can force the holder to prove subdomains once.
  3. **Proven umbrella**: proving `a.com` via TXT restores free subdomains for that account regardless of contests; later claims by others no longer revoke it.
  4. **Other accounts' subdomains are never affected by the root's proof** (pending or proven) — the root owner may deliberately hand out subdomains, GitHub-style.
  5. **No override of exact conflicts**: if the root holder claims `b.a.com` while another account holds it, they must prove `b.a.com` itself (which transfers it).
  6. **Root lost**: if another account proves `a.com`, the loser's `a.com` becomes verified elsewhere and their free subdomains drop back to awaiting proof.
  - Why the contest trigger covers the whole root, not just `a.com`: an owner using only `shop.a.com` (+ a `*.a.com` wildcard at our edge) never claims bare `a.com`, so an attacker's free `promo.a.com` would otherwise never be unseated. In our flow the claim precedes the DNS instructions, so an owner's wildcard normally can't point at us before their first claim contests the root. Residual gap: an owner adding wildcard DNS *before* any fortropp claim under that root while an attacker already holds it.
  - UI: no wording about inheritance (it confuses users who aren't already thinking about it).
- **Conflict = exact hostname only**: the hostname is held now, or was *ever* held, by a **different account** — including claims since removed, changed, or whose website/account was deleted. Requires a permanent tombstone of every hostname ever claimed, per account, with no time-based expiry (dangling CNAMEs live for years). Same-account claims aren't conflicts: conflicts exist to protect *other* accounts' claims, not because owning one hostname implies owning another (it doesn't).
- **Passing verification on a conflicted hostname transfers it**; the previous holder loses it. Verification always overrides tombstones (domains expire and get resold).
- **Never reuse site IDs** — the TXT token is bound to the website ID, and the `<site-id>` address follow-up below relies on IDs being unique forever.
- **Dashboard setup guide**: a gated domain shows two copyable instructions — "point it at us" (shared edge name) and "add this TXT record to prove it's yours" — then a Check action.
- **Why not a sibling/registrable-domain conflict rule** (considered, rejected): conflicts only get checked when a claim is made, so order matters. An attacker claims `a.b.com` before anything under `b.com` exists → no conflict; the owner later adds a `*.b.com` wildcard and verifies `b.com` → the attacker's site goes live through the wildcard. Always verifying subdomains closes this without a re-challenge mechanism.
- Threats covered: squatting (resolved by verification), dangling-DNS takeover after delete/domain change (tombstones + verified subdomains), wildcard takeover in either claim order (verified subdomains).
- **Schema (settled 2026-10-02, no prod data)**: `websites.domain` dropped. New `domains` (website_id FK cascade, hostname, status `pending_verification`|`active`, `verified_at`; partial unique index = one active holder per hostname, any number of pending claimants) + append-only `hostname_claims (hostname, user_id)` with no FK so it outlives accounts. Conflict check = `EXISTS hostname_claims WHERE hostname=$1 AND user_id<>$me`. Apex/subdomain derived from PSL at runtime, never stored. Live/cert status is Cloudflare's — queried, never cached in DB.
- **TXT token = HMAC, not stored**: `"v1." + base32(HMAC-SHA256(DOMAIN_VERIFICATION_KEY, "fortropp-domain-verify\0" || user_id || website_id || hostname))[..26]`. Version prefix allows key rotation; token isn't a secret (leaking it can only grant the domain to the token's own site).
- **KV split**: `host:<hostname>` → website_id (written on activate, overwritten on transfer), `site:<website_id>` → `{public}` (once per site, no drift across hostnames).
- **User-content domain**: the shared edge name (and the follow-up `<site-id>` addresses) live on a domain separate from the dashboard's (placeholder `fortropp.site`, `fortropp.test` in tests) — same-parent-domain user content could set cookies on the dashboard's parent domain and drag its reputation (cf. github.io vs github.com). Add to Public Suffix List later.
- **Follow-up (intended, not part of this item)**: serve every site at its own `<site-id>.<user-content-domain>` (github.io/netlify.app model) — wildcard DNS + cert routed to the Worker, Worker resolves site ID from hostname. Gives every site a working URL before any custom domain, and a real target for "Goto"/`public_gateway_url`.
- **Domain model (settled 2026-10-03)** — a list of domains per website, shown as a summary in the Delivery tab's second column with a separate manage page:
  - **No backend primary.** Every domain is on the TLS cert and in edge routing. The hostname shown in the switcher / profile table is the **first added** domain — a UI-only notion.
  - **Per-domain choice: serve the site, or redirect to a target** — each redirecting domain stores its own target, which must be a serving domain of the same site (Vercel's "Redirect to"). A redirecting domain costs the same as a serving one (verification, cert, routing, DNS pointed at us) — only the Worker's response differs. Removing a domain that others redirect to is blocked until they're re-pointed.
  - **Lost domains stay visible.** When another account wins a challenge for one of a site's domains, the row stays on the manage page marked as lost (not routed) instead of disappearing, plus an activity event for the loser. Needs a `lost` status instead of today's row deletion in `Domain::verify`.
  - **Setup tutorial only during addition**: Add domain → confirm → tutorial ("point it at us" + "prove it's yours" + Check) → finish → back to the manage page. The `www` offer is a checkbox in the add step, shown only when a root domain is typed; the tutorial then covers both hostnames.
  - **Resuming setup**: for a domain still awaiting proof, both a "Continue setup" item in its row menu and the "Awaiting proof" status itself (as a link-button) reopen the tutorial step — functionally identical.
  - **Lost rows** are labelled **"Verified elsewhere"** (implies proof, non-accusatory — often the domain was legitimately sold or lapsed). The label is a link-button opening a short explanation ("Someone proved they own this domain on another fortropp account on <date>. Reclaim it by proving ownership again."). Row actions: Remove (clear from the list) and Reclaim (new claim, needs TXT proof).
  - **Losing a redirect target**: domains that redirected to it switch to serve, with a note pointing at the lost row.
  - **One publish directory per website**, not per domain. Per-domain roots would blur "website" (that's a second website), make path-based redirect/header rules ambiguous, and split `publish` between `fortropp.toml` and the DB.
  - **`www` is not special.** After a root domain is added, the UI offers a one-click "Also add www.<domain>?" follow-up in context (Vercel-style; subdomains never see it, no permanent checkbox). Accepting just creates a second, ordinary domain entry, set by default to redirect to the root domain (doubles as a mini-tutorial: users see "www.a.com redirects to a.com"); it can be switched to serve like any other. If the root domain is still awaiting proof (held by another account), the `www` entry starts out serving, since redirect targets must be admitted. If the root is admitted but contested (no free subdomains), `www` still gets the redirect and awaits its own proof; the redirect takes effect once `www` is admitted. It counts toward the cap and is gated like any subdomain.
  - **Tutorial wording**: around the target hostname with the record type as a detail; a short "What is this?" link inside for users who don't know DNS. Pointing must be by hostname (CNAME, or ALIAS/flattened at the root), not A records: A records need fixed IPs, which on Cloudflare for SaaS means apex proxying (restricted to certain plans, likely Enterprise — unconfirmed), and hostnames let us move IPs without touching customers' DNS.
  - **Caps** (every resource gets a cap): hostnames **per account** — Starter 2, Growth 10, Scale 50 (`www` entries count like any other); pending claims capped at 5 per account. Per-account, not per-site: sites multiply (1/5/25 per plan), so per-site caps would allow up to 1,250 hostnames on Scale ≈ $125/month in Cloudflare for SaaS fees against a $19 plan. Only register a hostname with Cloudflare once it's live, never while pending. The per-plan hostname cap is listed on the homepage plan cards (homepage doubles as a list of promised-but-unenforced limits) and stays **unenforced until billing** gives users a plan.
  - Idea to explore separately: deliberately leaving users a way to feel they're "cheating the system" for a better deal, as an intentional marketing draw — but stay self-aware that it's never a genuine loss for us.

## [x] 6. Add API token auth + MCP server for agent-managed sites
Highest-alignment feature for the target market (users whose site was generated by Claude/ChatGPT, managing it via their agent rather than a dashboard). Most of the underlying capability already exists — site_files upload/list/delete endpoints (`backend/src/api/files/site_files.rs`), website create/update (`backend/src/api/websites.rs`). The gap: `backend/src/auth.rs` is session/cookie-only (`AuthedUser` via `FromRequest` reads a session) — no bearer/API-token auth path exists, and an MCP server/agent can't hold a browser session. Scope: (1) API token issuance + a token-based `AuthedUser` variant alongside the existing session one, (2) an MCP server exposing tools wrapping the existing website/file endpoints (create site, upload file(s), set public, list files, etc.). Cheaper to build than the CI-receive pipeline (item 7) and more directly matches the product's positioning — most LLM-generated static sites have no build step at all, so direct agent-to-fortropp push beats routing through git/CI for this audience.

Notes (2026-10-04):
- **No LLM cost to us**: the MCP server runs no model; the user's own AI client spends their own tokens and only calls our deterministic tools. Our per-call cost is ordinary API work (DB + R2/KV).
- **Auth options for account-acting tools**: pasted API keys (simple, rough for non-technical users) vs OAuth sign-in for a remote MCP connector (user clicks "Allow", never handles a token; backend acts as an OAuth server). A read-only knowledge server (docs, `fortropp.toml` help) needs no auth and could ship first.
- **Cap API tokens / OAuth grants per account** (unbounded tokens are a security issue). Exact bound isn't critical, but:
  - store only a SHA-256 hash of each token with a unique index, so checking a request is one indexed lookup regardless of token count (~100 bytes per row);
  - use a fast hash, not argon2: tokens are high-entropy random strings, and a slow password hash on every API call would itself be a CPU DoS vector;
  - rate-limit token creation and token-authenticated requests (item 13).

## [x] 6.5. OAuth sign-in for MCP clients (agent opens a browser, human clicks Allow)
Split out of item 6 on 2026-10-05; becomes the **primary** way agents connect, with item 6's pasted API tokens as the secondary path for scripts/CI (item 7). Goal: least possible friction — the user's agent adds `https://<api>/api/mcp`, the client opens our consent page ("Claude Code wants to manage your fortropp websites"), the human signs in if needed and clicks Allow, done. The user never handles a token and may never know the API & agents page exists. This is the standard MCP authorization flow (spec 2026-07-28), already supported by Claude Code, Claude Desktop, claude.ai connectors, Cursor and ChatGPT — and the only option for claude.ai/ChatGPT connectors, which can't send custom `Authorization` headers (i.e. the non-technical target audience).

Scope (backend becomes a minimal OAuth 2.1 authorization server):
- `/.well-known/oauth-protected-resource` (RFC 9728) + `/.well-known/oauth-authorization-server` (RFC 8414); MCP 401s carry `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer resource_metadata=...`. These discovery docs are the only unauthenticated endpoints.
- Client identity via **Client ID Metadata Documents only, no DCR** (settled 2026-10-05, verified against client docs): client_id is an HTTPS URL to a JSON doc we fetch. Spec 2026-07-28 deprecates DCR in favour of this, and it avoids an unbounded registrations table (Anthropic notes DCR registers a new client on every fresh connection). Coverage: all Claude surfaces (claude.ai, Desktop, mobile, Code, Cowork), ChatGPT (`https://chatgpt.com/oauth/client.json`), VS Code. **Cursor is DCR-only** as of this writing → Cursor users paste an API token (item 6) until Cursor ships CIMD; no change needed on our side when it does. Cap/timeout/size-limit the metadata fetch (SSRF-aware: HTTPS only, no private IPs).
- **Claude-specific requirements** (from Anthropic's connector auth docs, stricter than the MCP spec):
  - Claude only picks CIMD when AS metadata has **both** `client_id_metadata_document_supported: true` **and** `"none"` in `token_endpoint_auth_methods_supported`; otherwise it silently falls back to DCR (which we won't offer → failure).
  - A `401` is required to start sign-in (`WWW-Authenticate` on a `200` is ignored); PRM `resource` must exactly equal the URL the user entered; Claude uses only the first `authorization_servers` entry.
  - Redirect URIs: hosted Claude apps use `https://claude.ai/api/mcp/auth_callback`; Claude Code declares `http://localhost/callback` + `http://127.0.0.1/callback` and uses an ephemeral port → match loopback redirects **port-agnostically** for both hosts.
  - `/token` accepts `application/x-www-form-urlencoded`; dead refresh tokens → `invalid_grant`; rotate refresh tokens, new one in the same response.
  - Latency limits: 10 s for discovery/token, 30 s for refresh.
  - Advertise `code_challenge_methods_supported: ["S256"]`; `offline_access` in AS `scopes_supported` makes Claude request refresh tokens.
  - Consent screen must show the redirect URI hostname, with an extra warning when all redirect URIs are loopback.
- `/authorize` → frontend consent page; PKCE S256 required, exact redirect-URI match against the client's metadata, `iss` in the authorization response, consent page not frameable (clickjacking).
- `/token`: authorization-code + refresh grants; short-lived access tokens, rotating refresh tokens.
- Rate-limit `/authorize`/`/token` and metadata fetches (item 13).

**Revised design (settled 2026-10-05, after review)** — types first, invalid data can't deserialize:
- **Secret kinds**: one `Secret<K>` / `SecretHash<K>` with distinct prefixes — `fortropp_pat_` (pasted API token; migrates item 6's `fortropp_`), `fortropp_at_` (access), `fortropp_rt_` (refresh), `fortropp_ac_` (auth code). The bearer extractor dispatches on prefix; refresh tokens and codes can't be used as bearers at all.
- **Client identity**: `ClientMetadata` validates every field on deserialize (`ClientName`, 1–20 `RedirectUri`s, only `"none"` auth method); `RegisteredClient` is only constructible by the fetcher after checking `client_id` equals the document URL.
- **PKCE**: `CodeChallenge` (S256 only) / `CodeVerifier` newtypes, `challenge.verifies(&verifier)`. `Resource` newtype for the `resource` parameter.
- **Authorization request in two stages** (OAuth's error rules): stage 1 deserializes the fields whose failure must *not* redirect (`OAuthClientId`, `RedirectUri`, bounded `State`) → plain 400; stage 2 parses the rest into an `ApprovedRequest` proof type (private fields) or an `ErrorRedirect` built only from a matched redirect URI.
- **No pending DB state**: the auth code (Redis, 60 s, single-use) carries user, website scope, client, redirect URI and challenge; `PendingCode::exchange` does every check in one place. The grant row is created at code exchange; the consent page checks the cap read-only for a clear error, the insert enforces it.
- **Token request** is a `#[serde(tag = "grant_type")]` enum with typed fields; a form error handler maps parse failures to OAuth-format `invalid_request` (verify internally-tagged enums work with form bodies before relying on it).
- **Separate `agent_grants` table + `AgentGrant` type** (not the `api_tokens` table) — item 6 code stays untouched except the cap count spanning both tables and the token prefix. `ApiPrincipal` gets `credential: Credential::{Token, Agent}` with private fields, built only by the extractor or token issuance.
- **Refresh: no replay** — a reused refresh token gets `invalid_grant` and the client re-runs sign-in. Refreshing no longer kills the previous access token (it expires on its own, ≤1 h), which removes the main concurrent-refresh trigger; revocation kills all live access tokens via a per-grant Redis set. Upgrade path if users hit unexpected re-auth prompts: derive the next pair as `HMAC(server key, old refresh token)` so repeat refreshes are idempotent with no plaintext stored (rejected alternative: caching the plaintext response in Redis).

## Deploys design for items 7, 8, 9 (in progress 2026-10-08)
**Settled 2026-10-08**:
- **Storage is content-addressed, not `<website_id>/<deploy_id>/<path>`** (the guess in the suggested order above): copying a whole site per deploy would make a one-file edit on a 3,000-file site cost 3,000 R2 writes. Blobs live at `<website_id>/blobs/<sha256>`, stored once however many deploys use them.
- **A deploy is an immutable manifest**: path → `{hash, content_type}`, `fortropp.toml` included, so a rollback restores the config along with the content. A new deploy = the previous manifest + changes.
- **One upload path for dashboard, MCP and CI (item 7)**, Netlify-style digest flow: send the manifest of hashes → server returns the missing ones → upload only those → finalize.
- **Worker lookup**: `host:` → `site:{public, live_deploy}` → manifest (one KV value per deploy, immutable so cacheable forever, which helps item 11) → blob. Item 15's file cap bounds the manifest size.
- **Draft + Publish**: each site has one mutable draft with its own preview URL. Dashboard and agent changes land in the draft; Publish freezes it into a deploy and points `live_deploy` at it. Dashboard shows "N unpublished changes · Publish". This gives item 8 directly.
- **Rollback (item 9)** = re-point `live_deploy` at an older deploy. **Retention is a count per plan** (e.g. Starter 10 / Growth 50 / Scale 100, oldest pruned), replacing `weekly_backups`. GC deletes blobs no kept deploy or draft references. Storage cap counts unique blobs.
- **Preview access: anyone with the link**. The unguessable ID is the capability, so it works for private sites too (share before going public). Previews send `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`. Gated previews come later. Served at `<id>.<user-content-domain>` (same wildcard setup as the `<site-id>` follow-up in item 5).

- **Draft = pointer to its latest manifest** (`websites.draft_manifest_id`), no `draft_files` table. Each change batch reads the manifest, applies the changes, writes a new one and compare-and-sets the pointer (retry on conflict), so concurrent agent and dashboard writes don't lose updates. Publish = a `deploys` row pointing at the current draft manifest, nothing copied. GC deletes unpublished snapshots that are no longer current. The file listing is one R2 GET of the manifest instead of `ListObjects`.
- **R2 layout**: `<website_id>/blobs/<sha256-hex>` and `<website_id>/manifests/<manifest_id>.json` (`{path → {hash, content_type, size}}`). `content_type` lives in the manifest, not in R2 metadata.
- **Postgres**: `blobs(website_id, hash, size_bytes)` (missing-hash lookup, storage cap = `SUM`, GC candidates), `manifests(id, website_id, file_count, size_bytes)`, `deploys(id, website_id, manifest_id, preview_token)`, plus `websites.draft_manifest_id` (deferrable FK) and `websites.draft_preview_token`. `backups` is dropped once the dashboard step replaces its page.
- **Live pointer lives only in KV**, same rule as `public`, **one key per fact**: `site:<id>` = `{public}`, `live:<id>` = manifest ID (revised 2026-10-08 from a shared `site:{public, live_manifest}` record). A read-modify-write of a shared record loses updates on stale KV reads, and a Postgres row lock doesn't help against that: a public toggle reading a stale record would put back the old live manifest. Separate keys make every write a blind put. Cost: one more KV read per visitor request until item 11's edge cache. The draft preview pointer follows the same pattern.
- **Ceilings that don't depend on the plan**: 20,000 files per manifest (same limit as Cloudflare Pages), checked when deserializing; 100 kept deploys.
- **Branch `deploys`** in backend, frontend and dev-worker, merged at the end. Old `<id>/<path>` file endpoints stop working once the schema lands.
- **Preview URLs**: the draft has one stable link per site that survives publishes, and can be regenerated to revoke it. Every kept deploy is also browsable at its own ID, so a version can be checked before rolling back to it.
- **Agents may publish and roll back over MCP** with no human click. MCP uploads go to the draft; new `publish`, `list_deploys` and `rollback` tools are added. Tool descriptions tell the agent to share the preview link and get the user's OK before publishing. Rollback is the safety net.
- **No migration**: there is no production data, so dev and test buckets get reset.

**Build order**: schema → upload digest flow → worker lookup → dashboard draft/publish UI → MCP tools → previews → retention/GC.

## [ ] 11. Edge caching in the Worker (advertised on the homepage as "CDN")
The homepage lists CDN as a feature on every plan, but the Worker serves every request straight from KV + R2 with no caching. Every visitor request currently costs 1 Worker invocation + 2 KV reads (`host:` then `site:`) + 1 R2 read ≈ $1.68 per million requests; with Cloudflare's Cache API (free) a cache hit costs only the Worker invocation ≈ $0.32 per million. Scope: cache responses at the edge keyed by host + path, invalidate on upload/delete/public-toggle/domain change, and send sensible `Cache-Control` headers so browsers don't re-request assets on every page view (cuts requests per visit, which also matters for the visit caps in item 10). At Scale's 1M-visit cap (~20M requests) this is the difference between ~$34 and ~$6/month in request costs against a $19 plan.

- **Product caps come from Postgres** (item 12): hostnames per account, websites per plan, pending claims, storage and files per site are facts about what an account holds, so they are exact and durable, checked by counting rows inside the transaction that adds the resource (as the pending-claims cap already is), never in Redis.

Turnstile on all three forms makes the combination costly to automate. - [ ] **Replace, append or unset a header per subtree in `[[headers]]`.** Raised 2026-10-16 during the item 17 review.
  - *Problem:* a broad block (`for = "/*"`, `Bloop = "Foop"`) can't be switched off or added to below it. A more specific block with `Bloop = ""` wins but sends `Bloop:` with an empty value, which HTTP (RFC 9110) treats as present, not absent.
  - *Decided:* each block says, per header name, replace (today's default, via `values`), append (`append = ["Bloop"]` with the value in `values`) or unset (`unset = ["Bloop"]`). Resolution walks the matching blocks from least to most specific: replace overwrites, append adds, unset clears. E.g. `/*` sets `Bloop = "1"`, `/sub/*` appends `Bloop = "2"` → `Bloop: 1, 2` under `/sub/`; a deeper block can unset it again. Appending with nothing below is just a set. ## [x] Versioning rework (done 2026-11-05, releases 1–4: `v2026.11.04.6` to `v2026.11.05.3`)

- **Version:** an immutable snapshot of files, in one linear log. **Head** is the newest version. Creating a site appends an ordinary version named Init, so the log is never empty.
- **Files:** the working copy. **Draft:** exists exactly when `files ≠ head`; it is not stored. On screen, "draft" only ever means that state.
- **Live:** a nullable pointer to a version, recorded by version ID in Postgres, with KV `live:` as the worker's copy. It only moves explicitly. Visitors see a site exactly when live isn't null, on every host including redirecting domains; this replaced the Public setting and the `site:` key. A site with nothing live shows a "Last live" badge on the version that was.
- **Preview:** a link with a target, or none. The target is `head` (shows Files; "Latest changes" on screen) or one version, and only moves explicitly. Off is off: the link never works again.
- **Operations:** publish (append Files if `files ≠ head`; `live := head` if `make_live`), make live, take offline, discard (`files := head`), clone (refused while there is a draft; appends "Clone of {version}" as head), edit. None move the preview.
- **Writes are one deterministic line per website.** Publish names the files snapshot and revision; replace refuses to overwrite a draft unless unchanged or forced, and returns the new snapshot. Retention keeps the newest N plus live and preview.
- **Releases:** 1, backend foundation; 2, everything visible, Public merged into live; 3, contract: live-only worker, required publish inputs, replace refusal; 4, stop writing `site:`, typed publish effects. Leftovers belong to the launch rebuild item.

Generators (`Otp`, `PasswordResetToken`, built-in and guessed `ContentType`s, alongside the existing `DnsLabel`, `Secret`, `PreviewToken` and preview-host tests) are tested to parse back through their constructor rather than routed through it. Kept by design: `RegistrableHostname` and `RemoteHostname` check the Public Suffix List only under site-config's `public-suffix` mode (backend); serve-core builds with `trusted-wire` and accepts the hostnames the backend already checked, keeping `psl` out of the worker. Opaque Stripe IDs and card fingerprints carry no rules; the scaffold config is pinned by its round-trip test.
