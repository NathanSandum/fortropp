## Billing, requests and limits

- **Billing:** card required on every plan. Signup ends on the dashboard, which shows the plan step, with the plan picked on the homepage pre-selected. Until the subscription is active, the account has no plan, its caps are zero, and the dashboard shows the plan step. Starter accounts are deduped by Stripe card fingerprint: a card can only become a Starter account's card by passing our check, which refuses a card that is another live Starter account's current card. It runs on Starter signup, on a Starter account's card change, and on switching to Starter from a paid plan (against the card on file). A refusal offers a paid plan or a different card. An account frees its card when it changes card, moves to a paid plan, or is deleted. Paid plans aren't deduped. Reading the fingerprint before confirming (a ConfirmationToken's preview) is to verify. A speed bump, not a limit: wallets and virtual cards give fresh fingerprints. Then enforce every cap on the homepage plan cards (websites, domains, requests, storage, versions per site) plus files per site. Product caps are counted in Postgres inside the transaction that adds the resource.
- **Stripe shape:** the backend bills, Stripe charges; no Stripe Subscriptions, Products or Prices. Plan periods are rows in Postgres (account, plan, start, end once over); on the 1st of each month, the backend creates one Stripe invoice for the month before, with its charged lines, and charges the saved card; the invoice is tied to the month's line rows so a retry never charges twice. Monthly invoices are created as drafts with `auto_advance`; Stripe finalizes them, calculates tax and charges the saved card. Charge outcomes reach us through webhooks (`invoice.paid`, `invoice.payment_failed`, `invoice.finalization_failed`), so the webhook endpoint ships with the charging job. One Stripe Customer per user, created when the user first starts saving a card, so sign-up doesn't depend on Stripe. Cards are saved through SetupIntents with the Payment Element on our own page, styled through the Appearance API; nothing is charged at signup. No redirect-based payment methods (Klarna, Affirm and the like): SetupIntents and the Payment Element allow only cards (`allowed_payment_method_types: [card]`). No Stripe Customer Portal: our own billing page, built before launch, handles plan switching, card updates, cancelling and invoice history (Stripe's invoices and PDFs). Invoices are in USD, matching the expenses (DigitalOcean, Cloudflare, Postmark). Taxes are collected through Stripe Tax from launch, and every price shown includes them: invoice lines are tax-inclusive, so a user pays the same amount wherever they are and the tax comes out of it. Plan margins are worked out in USD, after the highest tax rate we collect. A USD bank account on the Stripe account keeps payouts in USD, with no conversion fee. Plans are sold only in the US and Canada at first. Starting a plan needs both an address in the US or Canada and a card issued in one of them (the card's country from the confirmation token, checked before the SetupIntent), so the two never point elsewhere. The Add Card drawer collects country (US or Canada only) and postal code, set on the Stripe Customer with `tax.validate_location=immediately`, so Stripe Tax always has a location. Taxes: GST/HST (and QST if it applies) registered in Canada; no US registrations until Stripe Tax's threshold monitoring flags a state. Sign-up stays open everywhere. The drawer's fields, above the card form: "Country", a select with "United States" and "Canada" and nothing chosen at first, and "ZIP code" (United States) or "Postal code" (Canada). Refusals, under the form: a card issued elsewhere gets "Plans are available only in the US and Canada for now. Use a card issued in one of them."; a postal code Stripe can't place gets "Enter a valid ZIP code." or "Enter a valid postal code.". The current plan lives in Postgres, so cap checks stay inside the database transaction. Webhooks: invoice payment results (`invoice.paid`, `invoice.payment_failed`), disputes, refunds, early fraud warnings. Rust client: `async-stripe`. Tests: check Stripe's own tools (`stripe-mock`, the CLI's `listen`/`trigger`, sandboxes with test clocks) before building a fake. E2e tests that save a card run against the tests sandbox in their own recipe, `just backend::test-stripe`, through `op run` with the key in 1Password item `backend-api-tests`, like the real-API tests, so `just test` stays offline; webhook handling is tested with events signed with the test secret. The dev stack uses the dev sandbox: `just dev-up` runs through `op run` with the key in 1Password item `backend-dev`. Until the account is live, Stripe allows only one sandbox, so dev and tests share it and its key; they split into separate sandboxes once live. Stripe setup, in the sandbox and live: the restricted key needs Invoices Write and Payment Intents Write as well as what card saving uses (the sandbox key also has Events Read, used only by `just backend::test-stripe`; the live key doesn't get it); Stripe Tax needs a head office address, the default tax behavior set to Inclusive, and a default product tax code (Website Hosting, `txcd_10701100`).

- **Billing emails:** ours are sent for each failed automatic attempt (the month-end charge and Stripe's retries) and when an unpaid bill is paid and the sites come back, whether by a retry or from the page. A failed attempt from the page sends no email, since the page shows the failure; such events carry our API request's ID in the event's `request`, automatic ones none. Stripe's own failed-payment and payment-confirmation emails are off; its receipts for paid invoices stay on. Both emails use the existing email card. - **Deleting an account:** the modal asks, in order, for websites to be deleted, unpaid or due bills to be paid, and the plan to be cancelled. Any unbilled days are then charged to the card on file. Deletion needs DELETE typed, removes the account with its tokens, agent access and billing history, and emails the account.
- **Charging:** in arrears, nothing upfront. Billing follows the calendar month in UTC, like DigitalOcean. On the 1st, the user is charged for the month before at the plan's flat monthly price, the same for every month whatever its length. A month the plan covered only part of is charged by whole UTC days, counting the start day: price × days on the plan ÷ days in that month, rounded to the nearest cent. When a UTC month ends, and at every backend start, a backend job looks for ended months that aren't billed yet; a billing run then writes every user's lines for them and creates the Stripe invoices, a few at a time in parallel, so a charge lands about an hour after the month ends. A plan change charges nothing at once. It ends the old plan period and starts one on the new plan, with the day of the change counting for the new plan. Cancelling ends the plan at once. Each UTC day is billed to the last plan active during it, not counting time with no plan, so the day of cancelling is billed, and the day of a plan change goes to the new plan. Cancelling bills nothing at once; its days are billed on the 1st like any other. Only deleting the account settles up early. The bill on the 1st has a line per plan period in the month. A line under $1 is waived, never carried forward, and the user is told: the billing history marks it waived, and the Add Card drawer says so when the first month's line will be. A user can change plans once per calendar month; starting a plan and cancelling don't count. Each line is a row in Postgres covering a range of UTC days on one plan. Each range starts where the user's previous one ended, with a unique index on (user, range start), so every day on a plan is billed once. Starter's lines get rows too, at $0, so the history shows every month. A month with nothing to charge, whether Starter or every line waived, creates no Stripe invoice. The confirmation for a plan change shows the coming bill: "Your bill on December 1st will be $2.10 for Builder (November 1–9) + $13.30 for Entrepreneur (November 10–30) (UTC) = $15.40. You can change plans once per month." With a waived line: "On December 1st, your bill will be $18.37 for Entrepreneur, November 2–30 (UTC), and the partial period on Builder, November 1, is waived. You can change plans once per month." Wording for the waiver in the billing history and the drawer to show before it's written. The Stripe account's time zone is UTC. No credits or refunds. Collection risk is accepted. The first failed charge puts the account into shutdown: its sites stop serving and nothing can be added. Days spent shut down aren't billed: the day rule counts shutdown like time with no plan, so billing stops while the sites are offline and resumes once the bill is paid. Stripe's retry schedule for one-off invoices sets the window: retries on days 1, 3 and 7 after the failure (custom rules of 1, 2 and 4 days after the previous attempt). A successful retry, or paying on our billing page (with the card on file or a new one), which charges the open invoice at once, restores the account. When Stripe's retries run out, the account stays shut down, with everything kept, until the bill is paid on our billing page. The invoice stays open so it can still be paid. Stripe doesn't retry hard declines on its own, but the schedule keeps running. Each failure sends an email saying the sites are offline and how to pay; wording to show before it's written. Deleting an account never deletes its Stripe Customer, invoices or charges: Stripe keeps the billing records the law requires (6 years in Canada), with the Customer's `user_id` metadata linking them to the account. The `bills` and `bill_lines` rows go with the account. Deleting an account first bills its unbilled days in the current month, as if the month ended that day, and charges the saved card.
- **Requests:** every request to a website's hostnames or preview link counts as one, whatever the answer: files, redirects, proxied replies, 404s, the unavailable page. The worker writes an Analytics Engine data point per request, carrying the account and the website. The account comes from the account ID in the host and preview routing records, so a deleted website's requests still count for the month. Requests that reach no website are recorded with no account. Counted per calendar month in UTC, per account across all its websites; the dashboard shows when the count resets in the viewer's local time. A check is needed because an account crosses its cap through visitor traffic alone, with no event to react to. The backend's Cloudflare token gains Analytics read. The plan cards say "Requests/mo". The caps stay 10k, 100k and 1M until margins are worked out at the end of billing. ## Product

- **Design: the per-domain label as ownership proof (launch, before domain setup over MCP).** A claim proves its hostname when DNS points at that claim's own `<label>.edge.fortropp.app`, replacing the TXT record for a one-record setup; whoever's label the DNS points at now holds the name, so re-adding never needs proof again. Subdomains: the backend follows the CNAME chain to the claim's label before admitting and routing (today the wildcard and Cloudflare accept any name in the zone, and serving keys on `Host`). Apexes: the port-80 challenge only shows that the name points at some pending override, since every override shares `ORIGIN_IPV4`, so at most one claim per apex may hold an override, with a way out for a squatted slot (expiry, or TXT for contested apexes). Labels are never reissued (`dns_label` is unique among live rows only). To decide: when proof is re-checked and what happens when DNS moves to another claim's label; a fallback for customers on Cloudflare DNS, whose proxied CNAMEs and flattened apexes hide the chain; what remains of tombstones, contests and umbrellas.
- **Suggest registrars with ALIAS when someone needs a domain (idea).** Porkbun or Namecheap, possibly with referral income. Don't name Cloudflare.
- **Marketing consent on sign-up (launch).** An opt-in on the sign-up page, starting unchecked, with the consent and when it was given stored in the database. Sending campaigns comes later.
- **Stop recording sign-ins as activity (maybe).** They clutter the operator's own account during testing, but a sign-in history is how a user notices one that wasn't theirs; revisit once testing stops. Recorded only in `backend/src/api/auth/sign_in.rs`, message in `EventLog::signed_in`.
- **Status while awaiting proof (to discuss).** In "Set up your domain", a domain awaiting proof (every subdomain, such as `www`) shows its CNAME and TXT records but no status line or "Show current status" popover, which only appear once it is admitted and waiting for DNS (the popover shows Cloudflare's hint, which exists only after registration). Until Check is pressed, nothing says what the proof lookup sees.
- **Support page (launch).** A minimal rich-text editor whose message is emailed to `support@fortropp.dev` through Postmark, with `Reply-To` set to the user's address. It also carries what a support tool needs to sort messages: a category picked by the user, plus the account and website filled in automatically. The support tool itself is undecided. Capped like every resource: message length, and a per-user rate limit.
- **A visual presentation round before launch.** Document the user flows and work through the friction found in them. Includes Delivery's layout for first-time visitors, the page-consistency audit against Profile's pattern, how to explain `www` and bare domains, and whether a first-time tutorial page would help (maybe).
- **Tell old dashboard tabs a new version is out (launch).** The backend already compares the frontend's `x-fortropp-client` header with its build (`build_version.rs`) and only logs a mismatch; it also returns its build version, and the frontend shows a toast with a Refresh action when they differ. Wording to confirm. Not by wiping sessions.
- **Domain setup over MCP (launch).** Easy and safe for an agent to do most of it, after the domain flow's friction is removed. Add, show records, verify, serve/redirect, reclaim; decide which destructive actions an agent may take. Reuse the domain logic; explain DNS state in plain words. Update `/docs/agents`.
- **Range requests in the worker (launch; research first).** Safari and every iOS browser refuse to play `<video>` from a server without ranges, so even a short clip on a site breaks on iPhones; also audio, in-browser PDFs and resumable downloads. Video streaming (transcoding, HLS) is out of scope: users embed a video service, and `MAX_FILE_BYTES` (25 MB) rules out long videos. Single vs multiple ranges, `416`, `If-Range` with ETags (the blob hash is a natural one), R2's ranged `get`, edge caching, and the Rocq model.
- **Admin app.** A native macOS app in Swift that signs into the backend to monitor things. Dev builds can also trigger test payments, to exercise billing flows such as failed and fraudulent charges by hand.
- **Edge caching in the Worker (launch).** Advertised as "CDN" on the homepage. Cloudflare's Cache API keyed by host + path, invalidated on changes, with `Cache-Control` headers. Weigh with KV `cacheTtl` and the benchmark.

- **Benchmark the worker on real Cloudflare.** Cold start and request latency. Current wasm: 763 KB raw, 258 KB gzip.
- **Redesign the manifest for partial reads, and bound the worker's cache by bytes.** Today one JSON object per version (`serve-core/src/manifest.rs`: every file entry plus the compiled `fortropp.toml`), cached 64 per isolate by count (`worker/src/manifest_cache.rs`) while a site can have 20,000 files. The resolver only does point lookups, so the manifest can split: the config as its own object, and the file index in content-addressed shards, so publishes reuse unchanged shards. The worker then caches configs and shards in a byte-capped cache driven by what visitors request (idea: user). Fold in binary manifests (postcard: measure decode time first, prefix a format version byte, keep KV records JSON); signed manifests would sign shards. Design with edge caching; measure in the benchmark.
- **Every route has a rate limit.** Per user on signed-in routes and per IP on the rest, so no one can wear down the backend or the shared Cloudflare quotas. Decide per route group, with generous defaults and tighter limits where a request costs a Cloudflare call or KV read. Session endpoints such as `/state` spend shared KV and R2 quota with no limit today, and exhausting KV takes down every site; weigh fewer KV reads alongside. Weigh moving the per-IP layer into Caddy (`caddy-ratelimit`, a custom image) once a second route needs it.
- **Observability (launch).** The backend's `tracing` exports through an OpenTelemetry subscriber to a collector on the droplet, which sends traces and logs to Grafana Cloud, so an incident reference is a search. Turn "client version differs" into a counter. Grafana Cloud becomes a processor of logged data: add it to the privacy policy's list.
- **Routes shaped around the frontend.** Review the interface between frontend and backend so the backend has a route for each page and typical user flow, bundling what that page or flow needs into one call. The finer-grained routes stay, for less predictable use and for development flexibility. Harden it before launch.
- **Direct uploads for MCP agents.** MCP gains `plan_upload` and `commit_upload`, the dashboard's upload plan and commit, so agents that can make HTTP requests upload file bytes straight to R2 through presigned PUTs and the backend handles only hashes and metadata. Inline `upload_files` stays for tool-only clients (no HTTP), with its per-call cap lowered to small edits, and its description points larger uploads to plan/commit.
- **Versioned worker inputs.** Everything the worker reads that the backend writes carries a schema version: host, preview and live records in KV, and manifests and `fortropp.toml` in R2. The worker accepts the current version and the one before it, so a change ships as expand, migrate, contract: a worker that reads both versions, then the backend writing the new one and rewriting existing records, then a worker that drops the old one. Either side can then be deployed first. Today a new required field, such as `account_id`, means the backend and rewrite must go out before the worker, or every site stops serving.
- **A branch-coverage gate over the Rust ↔ Rocq corpus tests.** Before the manifest redesign and range requests touch the model, so the shared corpus is shown to cover them. The rest of the coupling work is post-launch. hax and rocq-of-rust spikes: `todo_archive.md`.
- **What the website switcher's subtitle shows.** The switcher shows each website's earliest-added domain under its name; no primary domain is defined. Decide what the subtitle shows, and drop `primary_hostname` from the API if nothing uses it.

## Context

- **Target market:** solo founder, not chasing Netlify and Vercel on features. The bet is UI simplicity and focus, for non-technical and AI-assisted site builders. MCP and agents are the headline differentiator.
- **Team seats:** cut, not a task.
- **Pricing:** Starter $0, Builder $7, Entrepreneur $19. Infra cost is cents to low dollars per customer. The threat is free tiers from below, so compete on simplicity, not price.
- **Why a Cloudflare Worker:** scale. If even a few hosted sites gain real traction, serving from our own machines would make us the bottleneck; Cloudflare's edge absorbs that traffic (throughput, TLS, DDoS) without one person scaling servers.
- **Database access:** every statement goes through `db::Db` (`backend/src/db/`). Clippy's `disallowed_types` refuses the pool, connections and transactions anywhere else, except at startup, in the MCP server, in the test-only routes and in the test-only request counts, each with its reason. A single statement takes a `Counted` executor, statements that must be atomic share a `Tx`, and code that mixes in outside calls (Stripe, Cloudflare, R2, Redis) takes `&mut Db`. The request extractors check what a route needs in one statement, standing included through their `Standing` parameter (`GrowAllowed`). `npm run test:e2e` writes `frontend/tests/e2e/db-traffic.txt`, each route's and MCP tool's peak statements and transactions, so a change in database traffic shows in the diff.
