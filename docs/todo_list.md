## Billing, page views and limits

- **Billing:** card required on every plan. Signup ends on Home, which shows the plan choice and card form, with the plan picked on the homepage pre-selected. Until the subscription is active, the account has no plan, its caps are zero, and the dashboard shows the plan step. Starter accounts are deduped by Stripe card fingerprint: a card can only become a Starter account's card by passing our check, which refuses a card that is another live Starter account's current card. It runs on Starter signup, on a Starter account's card change, and on switching to Starter from a paid plan (against the card on file). A refusal offers a paid plan or a different card. An account frees its card when it changes card, moves to a paid plan, or is deleted. Paid plans aren't deduped. Reading the fingerprint before confirming (a ConfirmationToken's preview) is to verify. A speed bump, not a limit: wallets and virtual cards give fresh fingerprints. Then enforce every cap on the homepage plan cards (websites, domains, page views, storage, versions kept) plus files per site. Product caps are counted in Postgres inside the transaction that adds the resource.
- **Stripe shape:** the backend bills, Stripe charges; no Stripe Subscriptions, Products or Prices. Plan periods are rows in Postgres (account, plan, start, end once over); on the 1st of each month, the backend creates one Stripe invoice for the month before, with its charged lines, and charges the saved card; the invoice is tied to the month's line rows so a retry never charges twice. Monthly invoices are created as drafts with `auto_advance`; Stripe finalizes them, calculates tax and charges the saved card. Charge outcomes reach us through webhooks (`invoice.paid`, `invoice.payment_failed`, `invoice.finalization_failed`), so the webhook endpoint ships with the charging job. One Stripe Customer per user, created when the user first starts saving a card, so sign-up doesn't depend on Stripe. Cards are saved through SetupIntents with the Payment Element on our own page, styled through the Appearance API; nothing is charged at signup. No redirect-based payment methods (Klarna, Affirm and the like): SetupIntents and the Payment Element allow only cards (`allowed_payment_method_types: [card]`). No Stripe Customer Portal: our own billing page, built before launch, handles plan switching, card updates, cancelling and invoice history (Stripe's invoices and PDFs). Invoices are in USD, matching the expenses (DigitalOcean, Cloudflare, Postmark). Taxes are collected through Stripe Tax from launch, and every price shown includes them: invoice lines are tax-inclusive, so a user pays the same amount wherever they are and the tax comes out of it. Plan margins are worked out in USD, after the highest tax rate we collect. A USD bank account on the Stripe account keeps payouts in USD, with no conversion fee. Plans are sold only in the US and Canada at first. Starting a plan needs both an address in the US or Canada and a card issued in one of them (the card's country from the confirmation token, checked before the SetupIntent), so the two never point elsewhere. The Add Card drawer collects country (US or Canada only) and postal code, set on the Stripe Customer with `tax.validate_location=immediately`, so Stripe Tax always has a location. Taxes: GST/HST (and QST if it applies) registered in Canada; no US registrations until Stripe Tax's threshold monitoring flags a state. Sign-up stays open everywhere. The drawer's fields, above the card form: "Country", a select with "United States" and "Canada" and nothing chosen at first, and "ZIP code" (United States) or "Postal code" (Canada). Refusals, under the form: a card issued elsewhere gets "Plans are available only in the US and Canada for now. Use a card issued in one of them."; a postal code Stripe can't place gets "Enter a valid ZIP code." or "Enter a valid postal code.". The current plan lives in Postgres, so cap checks stay inside the database transaction. Webhooks: invoice payment results (`invoice.paid`, `invoice.payment_failed`), disputes, refunds, early fraud warnings. Rust client: `async-stripe`. Tests: check Stripe's own tools (`stripe-mock`, the CLI's `listen`/`trigger`, sandboxes with test clocks) before building a fake. E2e tests that save a card run against the tests sandbox in their own recipe, `just backend::test-stripe`, through `op run` with the key in 1Password item `backend-api-tests`, like the real-API tests, so `just test` stays offline; webhook handling is tested with events signed with the test secret. The dev stack uses the dev sandbox: `just dev-up` runs through `op run` with the key in 1Password item `backend-dev`. Until the account is live, Stripe allows only one sandbox, so dev and tests share it and its key; they split into separate sandboxes once live.
- **Dashboard without a subscription:** a new Home tab in the sidebar for `/dashboard`, which the UI can't reach once left today. While there is no subscription, Home shows the plan step: "Confirm your plan" above three plan cards, each with its name, icon, price, caps table and a "Choose …" button. Choosing opens the Add Card drawer on the right, with the plan, its billing line and the card form. Paid plans' billing line says when the first charge comes and how much. The charge date is in the viewer's local time, and the partial period's days are in UTC: "Nothing is charged today. On December 1st, you'll be charged $4.43 for the partial period November 12–30 (UTC), and $7 on the 1st of each month after that." For a plan started on the 1st (UTC): "Nothing is charged today. Your first $7 charge will be on December 1st, and then on the 1st of each month after that." West of UTC, the charge falls on the evening of the month's last day, so the line says "November 30th" and "the last day of each month". Starter's: "We ask for a card to prevent abuse; it's never charged while you're on Starter." Once the card is saved, the drawer shows "You're all set" and closes, and Home fades to the `Home` component. The plan picked on the homepage is kept in local storage and pre-selected, otherwise Starter. Files, Delivery and Analytics stay greyed out while the account has no website, as today, and a `(subscription)` route group around their routes redirects to Home when they're reached by URL without a plan. Support and the account pages (Profile, Billing, API & agents) stay reachable. Creating websites is stopped by the caps, which are zero without a plan, not by the UI. Once subscribed, Home shows a `Home` component holding today's `/dashboard` content ("No websites yet" when there are none, otherwise blank), to be filled in later. With this UI work, the plan cards switch from visits to page views: "Page views/mo" in `Offer.svelte`, and `max_thousand_vists`/`formatVisits` renamed in `Offering.ts` and `Offer.svelte`.
- **Downgrades:** refused until the account fits the new plan's caps; the billing page lists what's over ("Remove 1 website and 7 GB of storage to switch to Builder"). Page views don't block a downgrade; an account over its new page-view cap gets the usual warning. The check holds the same lock as adding a resource, and the lower plan's caps apply from that moment, before Stripe's webhook confirms, so nothing can be added in between.
- **Charging:** in arrears, nothing upfront. Billing follows the calendar month in UTC, like DigitalOcean. On the 1st, the user is charged for the month before at the plan's flat monthly price, the same for every month whatever its length. A month the plan covered only part of is charged by whole UTC days, counting the start day: price × days on the plan ÷ days in that month, rounded to the nearest cent. A backend job runs every 6 hours, finds months that have ended and aren't charged yet, and charges them, so a charge lands up to 6 hours after the month ends. A plan change charges nothing at once. It ends the old plan period and starts one on the new plan, with the day of the change counting for the new plan. Cancelling ends the plan at once, but the day of cancelling is billed: the plan period's end is set to the end of that UTC day, so its line covers the cancel day. The bill on the 1st has a line per plan period in the month. A line under $1 is waived, never carried forward, and the user is told: the billing history marks it waived, and the Add Card drawer says so when the first month's line will be. A user can change plans once per calendar month; starting a plan and cancelling don't count. Each line is a row in Postgres covering a range of UTC days on one plan. Each range starts where the user's previous one ended, with a unique index on (user, range start), so every day on a plan is billed once. Starter's lines get rows too, at $0, so the history shows every month. A month with nothing to charge, whether Starter or every line waived, creates no Stripe invoice. The confirmation for a plan change shows the coming bill: "Your bill on December 1st will be $2.10 for Builder (November 1–9) + $13.30 for Entrepreneur (November 10–30) (UTC) = $15.40. You can change plans once per month." With a waived line: "On December 1st, your bill will be $18.37 for Entrepreneur, November 2–30 (UTC), and the partial period on Builder, November 1, is waived. You can change plans once per month." Wording for the waiver in the billing history and the drawer to show before it's written. The Stripe account's time zone is UTC. No credits or refunds. Collection risk is accepted. The first failed charge puts the account into shutdown: its sites stop serving and nothing can be added. Stripe's retry schedule for one-off invoices sets the window: retries on days 1, 3 and 7 after the failure (custom rules of 1, 2 and 4 days after the previous attempt). A successful retry, or paying on our billing page (with the card on file or a new one), which charges the open invoice at once, restores the account. Stripe's last failed retry (`invoice.payment_failed` with no next attempt) deletes every website on the account, with its files, versions and domains, and sets the account's plan to none; no timer of ours. Stripe doesn't retry hard declines on its own, but the schedule keeps running. Each failure sends an email saying the sites are offline, how many days are left and how to pay; wording to show before it's written. The unpaid invoice is written off (marked uncollectible in Stripe). Tokens and agent grants scoped to a deleted website go with it; account-wide ones and the activity history stay. The deletion is recorded as an activity event; wording to show before it's written. Deleting an account never deletes its Stripe Customer, invoices or charges: Stripe keeps the billing records the law requires (6 years in Canada), with the Customer's `user_id` metadata linking them to the account. The `bills` and `bill_lines` rows go with the account. Deleting an account first bills its unbilled days in the current month, as if the month ended that day, and charges the saved card.
- **Page views:** a GET answered with an HTML page, tagged by the worker in its Analytics Engine data point. Every request is recorded too, so page views can be related to request costs once data comes in. Counted: HTML from a file, a rewrite, a proxy rule or a custom 404 page, on live hosts and preview links alike; redirects that leave the website (rules pointing at a full URL). Not counted: relative redirects, domain redirects, 304s, plain-text not-found replies. Counted per calendar month in UTC, per account across all its websites; the dashboard shows when the count resets in the viewer's local time. At the cap (launch): the user is warned with an upgrade prompt, and the sites keep serving. The warning fires at 100% only, in two places. A dashboard banner, from a query of the month's page views when the dashboard loads. And one email per account per month, sent by a daily backend job that asks Analytics Engine for every account's total in one grouped query and records each email sent in Postgres (the reason for the job: an account crosses its cap through visitor traffic alone, with no event to react to). The backend's Cloudflare token gains Analytics read. Banner and email wording to show before it's written.

Candidate source: Workers Analytics Engine (the worker writes a data point per request; the backend queries it). Users wanting more can add their own analytics script to their site (e.g. Umami). ## Product

- **Design: the per-domain label as ownership proof (launch, before domain setup over MCP).** A claim proves its hostname when DNS points at that claim's own `<label>.edge.fortropp.app`, replacing the TXT record for a one-record setup; whoever's label the DNS points at now holds the name, so re-adding never needs proof again. Subdomains: the backend follows the CNAME chain to the claim's label before admitting and routing (today the wildcard and Cloudflare accept any name in the zone, and serving keys on `Host`). Apexes: the port-80 challenge only shows that the name points at some pending override, since every override shares `ORIGIN_IPV4`, so at most one claim per apex may hold an override, with a way out for a squatted slot (expiry, or TXT for contested apexes). Labels are never reissued (`dns_label` is unique among live rows only). To decide: when proof is re-checked and what happens when DNS moves to another claim's label; a fallback for customers on Cloudflare DNS, whose proxied CNAMEs and flattened apexes hide the chain; what remains of tombstones, contests and umbrellas.

## Context

- **Target market:** solo founder, not chasing Netlify and Vercel on features. The bet is UI simplicity and focus, for non-technical and AI-assisted site builders. MCP and agents are the headline differentiator.
- **Team seats:** cut, not a task.
- **Pricing:** Starter $0, Builder $7, Entrepreneur $19. Infra cost is cents to low dollars per customer. The threat is free tiers from below, so compete on simplicity, not price.
- **Why a Cloudflare Worker:** scale. If even a few hosted sites gain real traction, serving from our own machines would make us the bottleneck; Cloudflare's edge absorbs that traffic (throughput, TLS, DDoS) without one person scaling servers.
